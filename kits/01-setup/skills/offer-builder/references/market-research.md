# Market Research Playbook

How to run Steps 1–5 well for any industry.

## Contents
1. Identify the business type
2. Demographics & psychographics checklists

4. Competitor research: where and how to search

## 1. Identify the business type first

The business type changes who the buyer is, where competitors are found, and how risk can be killed.

| Type | Examples | Key buyer question |
|---|---|---|
| Physical consumer product | food, beauty, fashion, home goods | "Is it worth the price, and will I like it?" |
| Food & beverage / restaurant | café, home bakery, catering | "Is it good, convenient, and worth the trip/delivery?" |
| Personal service | salon, fitness, tutoring, cleaning | "Will they do a good job for me, and is it a hassle?" |
| Health / wellness / aesthetic | clinic, spa, supplements | "Is it safe, will it work, can I trust them?" |
| Coaching / courses / education | online course, coaching, workshop | "Will this work for someone like me, and do I have time?" |
| Professional / B2B service | consulting, agency, accounting, legal, IT | "Will this pay off, and can I trust them with my business?" |
| Software / app / digital tool | SaaS, apps, templates | "Will it be easy to set up, and will my team use it?" |
| Real estate / big-ticket | property, cars, renovation | "Is this a safe, smart decision I won't regret?" |
| Local / brick-and-mortar | shop, laundromat, pet grooming | "Is it near, reliable, and better than the one I use now?" |

## 2. Demographics & psychographics checklists

**Demographics (B2C)**: age range, gender (only if it matters), location (city or area, urban/rural), income or spending bracket, occupation, education, civil status, kids, life stage (student, new parent, empty nester, retiree), language.

**Firmographics (B2B)**: industry, company size (employees and revenue), years in business, location, decision maker vs user vs approver, budget cycle, current tools or providers.

**Psychographics**:
- Values & priorities (health, family, status, saving money, convenience, sustainability, faith, career growth)
- Identity ("I'm a busy professional", "I'm a hands-on mom", "I'm a serious entrepreneur")
- Lifestyle & daily routine (where the product fits into their day)
- Buying behavior: impulse vs research, price-sensitive vs convenience-driven, brand-loyal vs deal-hunting, reads reviews, asks friends, follows influencers
- Media & hangouts: which platforms (Facebook groups, TikTok, IG, LinkedIn, YouTube, Shopee/Lazada, Viber communities), which influencers, offline places
- Triggers: what event makes them start looking (payday, a bad experience, a season, a life change, a deadline)

## 3. Finding pains, frustrations, hopes, and dreams

Good sources if you can search the web:
- Reviews of competitors, especially 1–3 star reviews (frustrations) and 5-star reviews (the results people celebrate)
- Reddit, Facebook group posts, forum threads, TikTok and YouTube comments
- Q&A on marketplaces (Shopee/Lazada/Amazon questions)
- "[product] reviews," "[product] complaints," "[product] worth it," "[problem] how to fix"

If you can't search, reason from the persona's daily life: what happens before they buy, during use, and after.

Prompts that help:
- What happens on a bad day because this problem isn't solved?
- What have they already tried, and why didn't it work?
- What do they fear will happen if they buy and it fails? (money, time, embarrassment, health, business risk)
- What would they brag about to a friend if it worked?
- Who do they want to become?

Write every item in the customer's voice, e.g., "Ang mahal ng salon pero after 2 weeks sira na ulit buhok ko," not "dissatisfaction with salon service durability."

## 4. Competitor research: where and how to search

Search for competitors **in the same market**: same location or channel, same category, and similar price tier. Run several searches, for example:
- "[product/service] [city]", "best [product/service] in [city/country]"
- "[product category] brand Philippines" (or their country), "[service] near [area]"
- Marketplaces: Shopee/Lazada/Amazon top sellers for the category
- Google Maps for local businesses (ratings and review counts)
- Facebook/Instagram/TikTok pages in the niche
- For B2B or services: agency directories, LinkedIn, Clutch, industry lists

For each competitor, look at: their headline promise, price, offer structure (packages, bundles, guarantees), proof they show, and what their reviews praise and complain about.

**Regional or local markets** (outside the capital, or small niches): general searches often return capital-city firms or vendor SEO pages. Try instead:
- Google Maps searches for the category + city, and read the ratings and reviews
- Facebook pages and groups for the city ("[city] business owners", "[city] buy and sell")
- Chamber of commerce, industry association, and local government business directories
- Local news sites, event sponsors, and expo exhibitor lists
- Searching in the local language or with local place names (district, mall, barangay)

Include indirect competitors when relevant:
- DIY ("I'll just do it myself / learn on YouTube")
- A substitute ("I'll buy the cheaper generic one")
- Doing nothing / status quo

If you cannot search, say clearly that the competitor list is based on general category knowledge, not live research, and ask the owner to confirm names and prices. Don't make up specific stats.

## 5. Positioning angles

Pick angles where (a) customers care, (b) competitors are weak or silent, and (c) the owner can truly deliver.

| Angle | Owns the idea of… | Works when… |
|---|---|---|
| Specialist / niche | "Made for [specific group]" | The market is crowded with generalists |
| Speed | "Fastest results / fastest delivery" | Customers are frustrated by slowness |
| Ease / done-for-you | "Zero effort for you" | Customers are busy or overwhelmed |
| Premium / luxury | "The best, worth it" | There's a status-driven segment and you can deliver quality |
| Value | "More for less, no compromise" | Price-sensitive market, but low quality is the common complaint |
| Trust / guaranteed | "Zero risk" | The category has many scams or bad experiences |
| Local / community | "From here, for here" | Local pride, proximity, or personal relationships matter |
| Results-proven | "Proof, not promises" | You have strong testimonials, numbers, or case studies |
| New category | "Not another X, a whole new way to Y" | You can genuinely reframe the problem |
| Personality / founder-led | "The person behind it" | The founder's story or expertise is a real differentiator |

A positioning statement template:
> For [specific target] who [pain/desire], [brand] is the [category/frame] that [key result], unlike [main alternative], which [its weakness].
